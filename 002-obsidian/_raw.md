There is a moment in every workflow where you realise a tool is not just useful, it is foundational. Obsidian was that moment for me.

Before making the switch, I was living inside Apple Notes. It worked well enough for quick personal reminders and short captures, but the moment content got complex, the cracks showed. Formatting would break, structure was hard to maintain, and anything I wanted to reference later was buried somewhere with no real logic holding it together. For light personal use, Apple Notes is fine. For building a serious knowledge system, especially one designed to work alongside AI, it simply was not built for that job.

Obsidian was. The difference was immediately clear. The seamless experience it delivers is not accidental. It is the result of a product designed around how information actually connects, rather than just how it gets captured. The folder structure, the linking between notes, the way everything sits in clean readable formats, it all adds up to something that feels genuinely intelligent in its design.

What changed everything for me was realising that Obsidian is not just a note-taking app. It is a notebook for AI. Whenever I need to reference anything inside a conversation with Claude, Codex, or any other model, it is all there, structured and ready. My phone and my Mac both have it installed, and the cloud sync means that wherever I am working from, I am always pulling from the same brain. That freedom to move between devices without losing context is something I did not fully appreciate until I had it.

What needs improvement
learning curve (12)

Obsidian asks you to think about structure from the beginning. You need to decide how you want to organise your folders, how you want to categorise your notes, and what system you are building toward. For someone who enjoys that kind of design thinking, it is one of the most satisfying parts of using the product. For someone who just wants to open an app and start typing without thinking, it might feel like homework at first.

But that structure is also exactly what makes it so powerful. Obsidian rewards the effort you put into it. The more intentionally you build inside it, the more useful it becomes, especially as a knowledge layer that sits beneath everything else you are building with AI. It has become one of those tools I cannot picture working without.

vs Alternatives
Apple Notes

Apple Notes was my previous home, and the comparison tells the whole story. Apple Notes is comfortable, native, and genuinely good for keeping a handful of personal things in one place. But it does not format content well for anything beyond simple text, it has no real folder depth for complex organisation, and it was never designed with AI workflows in mind. Once I started using Obsidian as the backbone of my AI note system, going back to Apple Notes felt like going back to sticky notes on a wall.

What's great
local storage (33)

The thing that made me stay is unglamorous: it is a folder of markdown files on my disk. Every other notes app I tried before this one, I eventually had to think about how I would get my notes out of it. With Obsidian that question never comes up, because there is nothing to get out of. They are already just files.

That sounds like a small thing until you have been burned once. I have moved machines, changed editors, and started reading my own notes programmatically from scripts, and none of that required Obsidian's permission or an export flow.

The second thing, which I did not expect: it turned out to be the best place to keep context for AI tools. Because it is plain text in a predictable structure, anything can read it. My agent setup reads the same vault I write in by hand. No sync layer, no API, no format negotiation. That was never a feature they advertised to me, it just falls out of the file-based design.

Where it is genuinely weak: search. It finds strings well and ideas badly. If I remember roughly what I thought but not what I typed, I am stuck browsing. And the plugin ecosystem is a real strength that is also a real tax, because a vault held together by nine community plugins is a vault that breaks in nine ways.

Still the tool on my list I would replace last.

What needs improvement

Search is the big one. It matches text, not meaning. If I remember the shape of a thought but not the words I used, there is no path from one to the other except scrolling. For a tool whose whole pitch is that it holds everything you know, not being able to ask it a fuzzy question is the gap I hit most often.

Second, the plugin situation cuts both ways. The ecosystem is genuinely why the tool is flexible, but a vault assembled from a dozen community plugins has a dozen independent maintainers and a dozen ways to break on an update. Some notion of a stable, supported core set would let people build on it with less anxiety.

Smaller: mobile is fine for reading and not fun for writing anything structured, so in practice my vault is a desktop thing that I occasionally glance at on a phone.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about pricing

Do I have to pay for commercial use?

No. You are not required to pay for a commercial license, however if you are using Obsidian for work in an organization we encourage you to purchase a commercial license to keep Obsidian independent and 100% user-supported. Learn more.

Can I buy Obsidian add-ons or licenses for someone else?

Yes! You can purchase Obsidian Credit for friends, family, and coworkers. This credit can be used to pay for any Obsidian license or service. To get started, create an account and buy credit. You can then send it to the recipient via email or a shareable link. Learn more.

What is your refund policy?

We offer full refunds within 7 days of purchase with no questions asked for Obsidian Sync and Publish. However, please note that Catalyst licenses, Commercial licenses, and Obsidian Credit are non-refundable. Learn more.

Do you store, access, or process user data?

Obsidian is a private and secure space for your thoughts. You can use our apps without sharing any personal information, and your data is stored locally on your device, making it inaccessible to us. Additionally, our apps do not collect telemetry data, and we never sell user data.

If you choose to use Obsidian Sync, your data is secured with AES‑256 end-to-end encryption, preventing us from reading it. Learn more.

Do you offer discounts for education, nonprofits, or other groups?

Yes, we do! Students, faculty members, and nonprofit employees are eligible for a 40% discount on Obsidian Sync and Publish. We do not offer any other discounts or promotions. Learn more.

What benefits does the Catalyst license grant?

By becoming a Catalyst member, you’ll receive early access to beta versions of Obsidian, as well as special badges within the community. Catalyst plays a vital role in helping Obsidian remain 100% user-supported, free from investor influence that could compromise our values.
