Figma is fantastic because it enables real-time collaboration and design iteration in a way that fundamentally changes how teams work together. Its browser-based architecture removes setup friction, while features like live co-editing, shared components, and design systems allow designers, developers, and product teams to stay aligned at all times. By unifying design, prototyping, and feedback into a single platform, Figma significantly improves speed, clarity, and cross-functional collaboration.

What needs improvement
slow performance with large files (20)
vector export issues (1)

While Figma is extremely powerful, performance can degrade in very large or complex files, especially when many components, variants, or collaborators are involved. Improving loading times and interaction responsiveness in these scenarios would significantly enhance the experience. Additionally, more advanced version control, clearer change tracking, and better permission granularity would help teams manage large-scale design systems more effectively.

vs Alternatives

We chose Figma because it enables real-time collaboration, reduces setup and tooling friction, and keeps design, product, and engineering teams aligned in a single shared workspace. Its browser-based approach, strong component and design system support, and seamless handoff to developers make it especially effective for fast-moving, cross-functional teams. Figma allows us to scale design workflows efficiently while maintaining consistency across products and brands.

Figma is one of the best tools for turning product ideas into something the whole team can actually understand.

What I like most is the collaboration layer. Designers, developers, founders, and product people can all look at the same flow, comment on details, and align before implementation starts.

For me, Figma is especially useful for landing pages, product flows, UI exploration, and developer handoff. It reduces a lot of back-and-forth because the visual decisions, spacing, components, and states are easier to discuss in one place.

What's great
real-time collaboration (176)
developer-friendly (20)
cloud-based platform (46)
design systems (27)
extensive plugin ecosystem (15)

Figma sits at the heart of everything we build. We run a web performance agency, and for us a design file is not a picture, it is a spec. Component libraries, spacing scales, responsive states and exact asset dimensions are all defined in Figma before a line of code is written, so developers implement what was actually intended instead of interpreting a flat mockup. That alone removes a whole class of rework.

The collaboration layer is what makes it indispensable rather than merely convenient. UX reviews happen straight in the file: a comment pinned to the exact element being discussed, in context, resolved once it is handled. It has replaced entire rounds of annotated screenshots and email threads with clients. Designers, developers and the client all work from the same source of truth, with nothing to install and no confusion about which file is the current one.

Dev Mode and the inspect panel make handoff fast, and the plugin ecosystem covers most of the remaining gaps. It is the one tool in our stack that nobody ever questions.

vs Alternatives
Adobe XD

Adobe XD was the main alternative on the table, and the comparison settles itself now. It always felt like a desktop-era tool with collaboration bolted on afterwards: file-based thinking, sharing as an export step rather than a default, and a plugin scene that never reached critical mass. We come across it less and less on projects, and nobody misses it.

Figma won on the thing that matters most to how we work: everything lives in the browser, so a client, a designer and a developer can be in the same file at the same time, with no install and no licence juggling. Add design systems that actually hold up across projects and a handoff developers trust, and there was never much of a debate.

What is a seat?

Seats give users access to Figma’s products. The different seats are designed to support different user workflows.

The Full seat includes full access to all Figma products, including Figma Design, Figma Make, Figma Draw, Figma Motion, Figma Sites, Dev Mode, FigJam, Figma Slides, Figma Buzz, and Figma Specs.

The Dev seat includes full access to Dev Mode, FigJam, Figma Slides, Figma Buzz, and Figma Specs. It also gives users view and comment access in Figma Design files.

The Collab seat includes full access to FigJam, Figma Slides, Figma Buzz, and Figma Specs. It also gives users view and comment access in Figma Design files. It only includes basic inspection on Dev Mode.

If you’re on a paid plan, you can also let others view and comment on your files without purchasing extra seats. This view and comment access only includes basic inspection on Dev Mode.

Where can I see a breakdown of my billing?

Only organization admins and billing group admins are able to view and manage billing. You can view a breakdown of your bill and manage seats from the Admin Dashboard in product. Learn more about billing

What happens if I add seats mid-year on an annual plan?

This depends on which plan you’re on.

If you’re on a Professional plan, if you add new paid seats to your team during your annual subscription term, Figma charges you a separate monthly subscription for those seats at the monthly price.

Once an admin approves a new seat, it will be automatically added to your next monthly invoice. The cost of the seat will be prorated based on the day it was approved until the end of your current monthly billing term.

You will continue to get monthly invoices for newly added seats unless you proactively add those seats to your annual subscription or remove the seats.

If you’re on an Organization or Enterprise plan, admins need to approve new seats that incur costs. By default, approved seats are included in your next invoice with prorated pricing, based on the date they were approved until the end of the annual subscription term. You’ll be invoiced quarterly for new seats added during your annual subscription term.

Can I purchase products individually?

We don’t offer the option to purchase products individually, but you can choose a plan and seats based on the products you’d like to use.

Can I inspect files without Dev Mode?

Yes, you can access inspect information as a free viewer. While you will have view-only access, you will be able to view measurements and property values, copy CSS, iOS, and Android code, export assets, comment on files, and collaborate via cursor chat. Learn more

What are AI credits and how do they work?

Figma AI uses a credit system that is shared across all Figma AI features and products. Every seat in Figma includes a monthly allotment of AI credits. The number of credits and which features you can use them with depends on your plan and seat type.

Full seats include:

3,000 credits/month on the Professional plan
3,500 credits/month on the Organization plan
4,250 credits/month on the Enterprise plan

All other seats (Collab, Dev, and View) and users on the Starter plan receive 500 credits/month.

If you need more credits, you can purchase additional AI credits on your plan. For more details, check out our Help Center article on how AI credits work.

Is Figma available for government or public sector teams?

Yes! You can learn more on figma.com/government.

How do I downgrade or cancel my paid plan?

If you’re on the Professional plan, you can downgrade or cancel your plan your Admin Dashboard. If you’re on an Organization or Enterprise plan, please contact our Sales team to downgrade or cancel your plan. Learn more

Where can I learn more about security on Figma?

We follow extensive security practices to to protect your data and privacy. All plans meet recognized international security, privacy, and compliance standards. Learn more on figma.com/security.

Do you have a discount for schools or classrooms?

Yes! Learn more on figma.com/education.
